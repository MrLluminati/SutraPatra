# SutraPatra · Devotional original-text provenance

**Scope:** Three initial source compositions released on 25 November 2026. This file records textual version choices, not modern editorial translations.

## Principles

1. Publish **historical original text** (or text independently transcribed from rights-cleared originals), never copy modern translation, commentary, phonetic guide or publisher-specific arrangement without permission.
2. Preserve a recognizable public traditional spelling; do not claim a single universally definitive reading. Explain meaningful variants.
3. Keep devotional statements distinct from medical, historical and scientific claims.
4. Retain source/variant notes in the public text and document provenance here.
5. Audio recordings, musical arrangements and performances may have separate rights. None are used.

## Hanuman Chalisa

- Traditional author: **Goswami Tulsidas**, sixteenth/seventeenth-century author (work in public domain).
- Text: 2 opening dohas, 40 chaupais, 1 closing doha; no added modern translation.
- Historical source witness: [Internet Archive — Tulsidas Hanuman Chalisa](https://archive.org/details/tulsi-hunuman-chalisa).
- Comparative textual references: [VedicDrishti source history](https://vedicdrishti.in/hi/devotion/hanuman-chalisa) and [Sanskrit Documents](https://sanskritdocuments.org/doc_hanumaana/hanuman40.html). **Sanskrit Documents prohibits commercial reposting of its volunteer-prepared file**. Do not copy its prepared file or website annotations.
- Variant decisions: older Awadhi spellings such as 'बिद्या', 'बिकट', 'चलीसा' retained. Some texts spell 'विद्या', 'विकट', 'चालीसा'.

## Om Jai Jagdish Hare

- Traditionally attributed to **Pandit Shardha Ram Phillauri** (died 1881); original song public domain.
- A readable historic print witness: [VedicDrishti source provenance and edition notes](https://vedicdrishti.in/devotion/om-jai-jagdish-hare), discussing an Arti Sangrah volume; refrain repetitions vary.
- Only historical underlying verses are reproduced, in a conventional accessible spelling; no modern prose translation or recording.

## Jai Ganesh Deva

- Traditional Hindi aarti, author **not conclusively established**. Do not claim that Surdas composed it merely because one version includes 'सूर'.
- Variant reference: [Sadhak — text comparison across editions](https://dailybhakti.app/aarti/jai-ganesh-deva/).
- Common four-stanza version is presented. Older aarti collections may replace the 'सूर श्याम' verse with 'दीनन की लाज राखो', or change stanza order.
- Do not imply this is the only traditional version.

## Future texts

Before adding another prayer, record: work title, author/era where known, public-domain or license basis, source edition, and variant choices. For works with uncertain modern authorship or insufficient republication rights, **do not publish the full text** until rights are clarified.

## शिव पंचाक्षर स्तोत्रम् / Shiva Panchakshara

- Sanskrit stotra: five verses corresponding to न, म, शि, वा, य; a separately marked phalashruti follows.
- Traditionally attributed to Adi Shankaracharya; the attribution is a *tradition*, not proof of authorship.
- Text witnesses: [Wikisource original](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/श्रीशिवपञ्चाक्षरस्तोत्रम्) and [Shaivam archive](https://shaivam.org/scripture/Sanskrit/ssk-shivapanchakshara-stotram/).
- Noted variants: `त्रिलोचनाय/विलोचनाय`, `मन्दारपुष्प/मन्दारमुख्य`. The page presents a familiar traditional reading with these differences disclosed.
- Only old Sanskrit source verses, not a modern translation or editorial commentary, are reproduced.

## श्री गणेश पंचरत्नम् / Ganesha Pancharatnam

- Sanskrit stotra: five numbered principal verses plus a separate phalashruti.
- Traditionally attributed to Adi Shankaracharya; avoid confident historical-authorship claims.
- Compare [Wikisource text A](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/गणेशपञ्चरत्नम्) and [Wikisource text B](https://sa.wikisource.org/wiki/गणेशपंचरत्न_स्तोत्रम्).
- Variants include `विलासिलोकरञ्जकम्/विलासिलोकरक्षकम्`, plus a misnumbered fifth verse in one transcription.
- No modern translation, performance, or arrangement is included.

## सुखकर्ता दुःखहर्ता / Sukhkarta Dukhharta

- Marathi Ganapati aarti, traditionally attributed to Samarth Ramdas.
- Primary-edition context: *Shriram Upasana Grantha* (1910) contains a three-stanza version, covered in [this source comparison](https://udyogjyotish.com/hi/aarti/sukhkarta-dukhharta).
- A shorter **two-stanza** text appears in an 1891 collected-works edition; see [the historical print comparison](https://www.livepanditji.com/blog/sukhkarta-dukhharta-aarti-meaning-ramdas/).
- The page presents the common **three-stanza** reading and does not represent it as the only version.
- Language: **Marathi**, in Devanagari script. This is not a Hindi translation.
